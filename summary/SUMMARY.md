## 2. Findings

1. **GW-EVA does not beat EVA (H2 not supported).** Under the shared protocol G5 − G3 is −0.15, −0.88, −1.23 and −0.46 points on SST-2, MRPC, RTE and low resource; every interval contains 0 and every mean is slightly negative. Only the step-0 part of H2 holds: GW-EVA's step-0 gradient on B is 1.3–2.1× EVA's (e.g. 3.36 vs 1.63 on MRPC). H2's other early-training claim, a lower loss over the first 500 steps, fails at equal learning rates: G5 and G3 are indistinguishable in steps 1–50 on every task, and on SST-2 G5's loss is slightly higher afterwards (+0.02, significant).
2. **On the full-data tasks, no initialisation beats plain LoRA.** All of G2–G6, G8 and G9 are within ±1.9 points of G1 on every task, and every interval contains 0. The spread between the best and worst of these eight groups is under 1.2 points on SST-2 and MRPC, smaller than the seed noise on RTE.
3. **Data-aware initialisations fit faster early on, but the advantage does not last.** On MRPC, where all groups share lr 2e-4, EVA, GW-EVA and LoRA-GA all have a significantly lower training loss than plain LoRA throughout the first 500 steps (−0.014 to −0.024 in steps 1–50, about −0.055 in steps 51–200), yet their final F1 is not significantly different. Matching only the magnitude (G2) moves the loss by 0.004 at most.
4. **A larger initial gradient does not translate into accuracy.** The step-0 gradient grows 7–37× from G1 to G3–G7. Matching EVA's row norm with random directions (G2) changes nothing; EVA's directions (G3) add a 4–9× larger gradient at the same norm, GW-EVA (G5) another 1.3–2.1×, and accuracy stays flat throughout. G8 even has a 1.4–1.7× larger step-0 gradient than G3 on MRPC and RTE while being slightly less accurate.
5. **With little data, EVA is worse than plain LoRA: most of the gap comes from the learning rate, the rest from EVA's directions.** Under the shared rule (low resource reuses the full-SST-2 learning rate) G3 trails G1 by 2.64 points, and G1 itself is insensitive to the learning rate (5e-4 and 1e-3 give the same result). EVA is not: lowering G3's learning rate from 1e-3 to 5e-4 and 2e-4 raises it from 89.50 to 90.55 and 90.87 (same GPU), so the shared rule gave EVA its worst setting. At its best learning rate EVA still trails G1 by 0.57 [−1.10, −0.05], a small but significant gap. Keeping EVA's directions but removing its rank redistribution (ρ = 1, exact budget) changes nothing (−0.41 [−2.32, +1.50]), so the remaining gap comes from the directions. The loss curves at lr 1e-3 show the mechanism: EVA and GW-EVA start level with LoRA and then fit the training set more slowly in steps 51–200 (+0.09 and +0.15, significant), an optimisation problem rather than overfitting. The earlier statement that the gap "is not a learning-rate effect" was wrong: the G1 control only showed that G1 does not depend on the learning rate.
6. **Allocating ranks by parameter cost (H3) has no effect on full data.** G4 − G3 and G6 − G5 are all non-significant on SST-2, MRPC and RTE. The one significant difference, G6 − G5 = +1.53 on low resource, is most likely the learning rate (footnote 2), and ρ = 1 vs ρ = 2 for EVA in low resource shows no allocation effect either.
7. **RTE training is fragile, and LoRA-GA (G7) is the worst case.** G7 averages 56.68 on RTE, 17.3 points below G1 and significant: seed 1 predicts one class for all 277 examples and seeds 2–3 are near chance; on SST-2 and MRPC it matches the others. Single-class collapse is not unique to LoRA-GA, though: with ten seeds, plain LoRA (G1, lr 5e-4) collapses on seed 8 (52.71, training loss stuck at ln 2), and at lr 1e-3 six of the seven tuned groups collapse to 48% on the RTE dev split. G7 differs in degree, with all five seeds below 67 against nine of ten above 70 for G1. RTE results should therefore be reported with the collapse rate, not just the mean.
8. **Where the calibration data comes from barely matters (H4).** EVA calibrated on another task (G9) or on generic wikitext-103 text (G8) is never significantly better or worse than plain LoRA on full data. Against EVA calibrated on the task itself (G3), the only significant difference is G8 on MRPC (−0.91 [−1.54, −0.28], with G8's small variance and no correction for the ~20 comparisons made here), where G8 also fits slightly more slowly early on (+0.007 to +0.015 loss). The subspaces themselves differ much more: G8 overlaps G3 by 0.64–0.72 and G9 by 0.49–0.66. G8, which keeps G3's token layout and only swaps the words, overlaps more than G9, which changes both the task and the format; most of the difference is on SST-2, where G9's calibration switches from single sentences to sentence pairs, so part of what looked like task content may be input format. Together with findings 1–4, this suggests that at r = 8 on RoBERTa-base, accuracy is insensitive to which directions A starts from.
9. **Low-resource results depend on the GPU seed by seed, not on average.** Rerunning G3 with the same code, versions and seeds on L40S instead of A40 changes single seeds by up to 2.5 points (the first training-step loss already differs, from bf16 numerics in the head warm-up), while the 5-seed mean moves by only +0.69 (not significant). Seed pairing across GPUs is therefore weaker in low resource; G8's +1.08 over G3 shrinks to +0.39 against the same-GPU rerun.

### Hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| H1: Σ_g's subspace differs from the activation covariance's | **Not measured** | No branch has the overlap between GW-EVA and EVA subspaces |
| H2: GW-EVA has a larger step-0 gradient, lower early loss and higher accuracy than EVA | **Not supported** | Only the step-0 gradient is larger; early loss equal or slightly higher, accuracy non-significant with slightly negative means (finding 1) |
| H3: cost-based allocation changes the result | **Not supported** | G4 − G3, G6 − G5 non-significant on full data; the low-resource G6 − G5 is most likely learning rate, and EVA with uniform ranks matches ρ = 2 (findings 5, 6) |
| H4: how task-specific the inputs are | **Answered for accuracy** | Task data, another task and generic text give the same accuracy; subspaces overlap 0.49–0.72 and may depend on input format more than content; no same-task noise floor yet (finding 8) |

Following the decision table in [`NEXT_STEPS.md`](NEXT_STEPS.md), the results point to the **analysis / negative-result** story: data-aware LoRA initialisations (EVA, GW-EVA, LoRA-GA) change the early gradients a lot and speed up early fitting, but leave the final accuracy unchanged on these tasks; in low resource they need a lower learning rate than LoRA and still end slightly behind. Whether the "subspaces differ" or "subspaces almost identical" branch applies still depends on H1.

## 4. What is missing

- **H1 diagnostic**: the overlap between the GW-EVA and EVA lora_A subspaces, with a same-task noise floor. The G5/G6 calibration statistics stay on the cluster (`outputs/gw_eva/shared_study/*/initialization/`); the repository only has their checksums. This is now the only open question that can change the story.
- **Same-task noise floor for the H4 overlaps**: EVA against itself on two disjoint calibration samples of the same task, so the 0.49–0.72 overlaps of G8/G9 can be read against it. The lora_A caches are git-ignored, so the overlaps cannot be regenerated from the repository.
- **Low-resource learning rate for G5/G6 (and G8)**: runs at 2e-4 and 5e-4 would show whether GW-EVA, like EVA, closes most of its gap to LoRA at a lower learning rate.
- **G5/G6 low-resource seeds 3–4.**
- **Why G7 collapses on RTE**, now that plain LoRA is known to collapse occasionally too.
- **Seeds 5–9 for G3 and G5** on MRPC, RTE and low resource, so the extra G1 seeds can be paired (G1 is done).

The plan for these is in [`NEXT_STEPS.md`](NEXT_STEPS.md).
