GW-EVA: does a gradient-weighted LoRA initialisation help?

We replace EVA's activation covariance with the gradient-weighted covariance Σg = E[‖g‖² x xᵀ], derived by dropping an independence assumption from EVA's optimality proof, and test it against eight other initialisations of LoRA's A matrix on RoBERTa-base. Every number below comes from the run files in the repository and is paired by seed.

01

Verdict on the four hypotheses

Data-aware initialisations change the step-0 gradient by an order of magnitude and speed up early fitting, but at a tuned learning rate none of them beats plain LoRA on final accuracy. GW-EVA is no better than EVA.

H1

Σg's subspace differs from EVA's

Not measured

The overlap between the GW-EVA and EVA subspaces, with a same-task noise floor, is the one open diagnostic.

H2

GW-EVA beats EVA

Not supported

Only the step-0 gradient is larger. Early loss is equal or slightly higher and every accuracy interval contains 0.

H3

Allocating ranks by parameter cost matters

Not supported

G4 − G3 and G6 − G5 are non-significant on full data; EVA with uniform ranks matches the original in low resource.

H4

EVA's subspace is task-specific

Answered

The subspace is task-specific (overlap 0.49–0.72 against a same-task floor of 0.92–0.95), but accuracy does not depend on it.

  1. No initialisation beats plain LoRA on full data at its tuned learning rate. G2–G6, G8 and G9 are within ±1.9 points of G1 on every task, and every interval contains 0.

  2. Data-aware inits fit faster early, but the gain does not last. On MRPC at equal lr, EVA, GW-EVA and LoRA-GA have a lower training loss for the first 500 steps and the same final F1.

  3. Larger initial gradients do not buy accuracy. ‖∂L/∂B‖ at step 0 grows 7–37× from LoRA to the data-aware inits while accuracy stays flat. The magnitude part matches theory: G2/G1 measures 1.78–1.83 against the predicted √3 ≈ 1.73.

  4. With 1k examples, EVA trails LoRA only because of the learning rate. At the shared rule G3 − G1 = −2.64; with both at lr 2e-4 on one GPU the gap is −0.57 and not significant.

  5. EVA helps on RTE when the learning rate is held fixed (+2.13 at lr 2e-4, 10 seeds), a robustness effect: LoRA needs a higher lr there, which occasionally collapses.

  6. GPU numerics shift single seeds by up to 2.5 points. Cross-GPU seed pairing produced an MRPC "edge" for EVA that disappears on one GPU.

05

Same-GPU and equal-learning-rate checks

Rerunning G3 on the GPU that G1 used, and putting both at the same learning rate, changes two conclusions: the MRPC edge disappears and an RTE advantage appears.

G3 and G4 ran on A40; G1, G2, G7, G8 and every control on L40S. The same code, versions and seeds move single low-resource seeds by up to 2.5 points between the two GPUs (bf16 numerics already differ at the first step), so only same-GPU pairs decide a finding.

Theory check: same-seed gradient ratios

For directions independent of Σg, the gradient scales with A's row norm: G2 (orthonormal rows) over G1 (Kaiming-uniform, row norm 1/√3) should give √3 ≈ 1.73. G3 / G2 isolates EVA's directions at equal row norm; G5 / G3 is GW-EVA's directions over EVA's. Mean (range) over seeds.
